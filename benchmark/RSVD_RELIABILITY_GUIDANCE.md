# rSVD reliability and validation

Randomized SVD is an approximate, seed-dependent direction solver. fastPLS
uses rSVD as its primary solver because it supports the CPU and accelerator
routes and avoids unnecessary full decompositions. PLS-SVD and standalone
`fastsvd()` use 32 oversampling directions and five power iterations.
Accelerated SIMPLS, OPLS, and kernel-PLS use 32 directions and five iterations
for ordinary shapes. High-response regression uses 48 directions and six
iterations when the response has at least 64 columns and the response-to-sample
ratio is at least 0.2. Sparse many-class classification uses 64 directions and
seven iterations when there are at least 32 classes and no more than 20 samples
per class. The 12/2 massive-matrix profile takes precedence when the explicit
cross-covariance would exceed 512 MiB.

## SIMPLS direction generation

CPU and Metal start each SIMPLS component from a new seeded oversampled
Gaussian sketch of the current deflated cross-covariance operator. CUDA can
generate up to eight fresh candidates together for large classification
workloads, but accepts them sequentially through the standard SIMPLS
orthogonalization and deflation equations. CPU supports explicit and
matrix-free operator products. CUDA keeps its large products and sketches on
the device. Metal performs the large sketch products on the GPU and the reduced
QR/SVD on the host.

## Qualification criteria

Against a CPU IRLBA fit using the same data, split, preprocessing, component
path, and prediction head, a randomized result is outside the publication
tolerance if any applicable condition holds:

- relative prediction or score error is greater than 0.01;
- the corresponding correlation is less than 0.995;
- a score, projection, or loading subspace angle is greater than 0.1 degrees;
- decoded-label agreement is less than 0.995; or
- the absolute predictive-metric difference is greater than 0.005.

These are numerical screening tolerances, not mathematical error bounds or
clinical equivalence margins. The controlled qualification varies sample
count, predictor and response dimensions, retained components, requested
prefixes, effective rank, class count, and cross-covariance storage over
multiple seeds. Its exact results are generated by `controlled_scaling/` and
must be reported with package version, controls, seeds, completed cases, and
failures.

The current-release controlled-scaling workflow records the effective controls
for every row and reports the number that meet each criterion. Results are
summarized only after all CPU, CUDA, and Metal runs for the same release are
complete; no result from a different control profile is relabelled as a public
default.

## User guidance

The fitted model records the solver, effective controls, seed, executed route,
and structural diagnostics. Repeating an important randomized analysis with
additional seeds remains appropriate when singular values are closely spaced
or conclusions lie near a decision boundary. CPU IRLBA is available for a
non-randomized iterative comparison when coefficients, loadings, or subspaces
are the scientific endpoint.
